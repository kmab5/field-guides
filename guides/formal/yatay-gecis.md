# A Guide to Yatay Geçiş for YTB Scholarship Students

If you are not satisfied with your current placement, you are not alone—and you may have options. This guide explains the steps involved in transferring universities as a YTB scholarship student. It is based on a transfer application that is still in progress, so it may be expanded and refined as more direct experience becomes available.

:::warning Set your expectations early
The probability of a successful transfer may be low. This is not meant to discourage you, but to keep your expectations realistic. Universities may be reluctant to take on the administrative work of admitting a scholarship student into an already established cohort. As you go through the process, keep alternative plans available and try to recognize the strengths of your current university as well.
:::

Personal experience is used throughout the guide as a reference point. Your own process may differ depending on your university, program, year of study, and the rules in force when you apply. Medicine and certain other programs may follow substantially different transfer procedures and are not covered in detail here.

## What is yatay geçiş?

tl;dr: The transfer process relevant to most YTB students is **kurumlar arası yurt içi yatay geçiş**. Use this phrase when researching universities and application rules.

*Yatay geçiş* translates literally to “horizontal transfer.” In Türkiye, student transfers are generally discussed as either **yatay geçiş** or **dikey geçiş**.

- **Yatay geçiş** usually means moving between programs or universities at the same level of study.
- **Dikey geçiş** is a different progression route and is not the process covered by this guide.

For the purposes of this guide, the goal is to move to another university while remaining in the same or an equivalent academic program—for example, transferring from Computer Engineering at one university to Computer Engineering at another.

| Transfer type | What it covers | Relevant here? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kurum içi yatay geçiş** | Transfer between programs within the same university | Usually no |
| **Kurumlar arası yatay geçiş** | Transfer between different universities | **Yes** |

*Kurumlar arası yatay geçiş* is also divided according to the location of the student’s current university:

- **Yurt içi:** transfer from a university in Türkiye.
- **Yurt dışı:** transfer from a university outside Türkiye.

For a YTB student already studying in Türkiye, the relevant category is generally **kurumlar arası yurt içi yatay geçiş**.

:::important Transfer student versus first-year student
Some universities may offer to admit you as a new first-year student and then recognize some of your previous courses. Although this can sound similar to a transfer, it is not necessarily the same administrative status.

For a YTB scholarship holder, registering again as a first-year student may extend the funded study period or create a semester loss. Apply for and obtain written confirmation of admission as a transfer student in the appropriate year or semester—not merely as a first-year student whose credits will later be evaluated.
:::

## When should you start preparing?

tl;dr: Most applications open after the academic year ends, often from late June through July or early August. Your academic results matter throughout the year, so preparation begins much earlier.

Unlike many universities that accept transfer applications throughout the academic year, universities in Türkiye commonly announce yatay geçiş applications after spring-semester grades have been finalized.

The main application period is often:

- late June;
- July; or
- early August.

You should nevertheless begin preparing from the start of the academic year. Your academic record is one of the most important parts of your application, and many universities require applicants to have passed all courses completed so far.

Transfers are generally relevant to students who have completed at least two semesters and have not yet entered the final stage of their program. The exact semester restrictions depend on the applicable regulation and the university’s own directive, so verify them before applying.

## What do you actually need?

tl;dr: Every university has its own requirements. Find and read the university’s yatay geçiş **yönergesi**, current application announcement, and document checklist.

Universities evaluate applicants using academic results, entrance or placement information, course compatibility, and other criteria stated in their regulations. The required documents and evaluation formula can differ substantially between institutions.

:::tip Always go to the source
The most reliable sources are the university’s official yatay geçiş directive, current application announcement, academic calendar, and relevant faculty or student-affairs pages. University rules can change, so do not rely only on third-party guides.
:::

The following documents are commonly requested.

### 1. ÖSYS results or an accepted substitute

Many university forms are designed for students admitted through Türkiye’s central placement system and may request an ÖSYS or YKS result.

International students may not have this document. Depending on the university, possible alternatives may include:

- **SAT results**;
- **ACT results**;
- a **national secondary-school examination** from your home country;
- your **high-school transcript**; or
- another document used for your original international admission or scholarship placement.

:::warning Confirm before submitting
Do not assume that every university accepts the same alternative. Ask which document an international YTB student should submit in place of an ÖSYS or YKS result.
:::

Some universities may assess applicants using the base entrance score, or *taban puanı*, of the destination program for the year in which the applicant entered university. They may also ask you to provide an official record of that score.

You can research program data through [YÖK Atlas](https://yokatlas.yok.gov.tr/), but you should still confirm how the target university applies that information to international applicants.

PS. The main research links are collected in the resources section at the end of this guide.

### 2. ÖSYS placement document or an accepted substitute

:::warning Accuracy not yet confirmed
The appropriate substitute may differ by university. Obtain written confirmation from the target university before relying on any particular document.
:::

Some applications request an ÖSYS or YKS placement document. A YTB student may need to provide documents showing the original scholarship and university placement, such as:

- the Türkiye Scholarships acceptance letter; and
- the **bursluluk belgesi** or another official scholarship document.

### 3. Transcript

Universities normally require an official, up-to-date transcript. Commonly accepted forms include:

- a barcode-verifiable transcript downloaded through **e-Devlet**; or
- a signed and stamped transcript issued by your university.

If the e-Devlet transcript does not show your latest grades, contact your university’s student affairs office (*öğrenci işleri*).

:::tip Check the issue date
Some universities require recently issued documents. The informal guide recommends obtaining the transcript no more than two weeks before the application, but the target university’s current announcement should be treated as the final authority.
:::

### 4. Öğrenci belgesi

The **öğrenci belgesi** confirms your current student status. It can usually be obtained:

- through e-Devlet; or
- as a signed and stamped document from your university.

### 5. Disiplin belgesi

A **disiplin belgesi** confirms whether you have received a disciplinary penalty.

It may be available through your university’s student portal. If it is not, ask the student affairs office for a disciplinary-status document specifically for a yatay geçiş application. In some universities, this may be issued as an öğrenci belgesi containing an additional statement that the student has no disciplinary record.

### 6. Kimlik

Provide a clear scan of your Turkish identity or residence document, according to the application instructions.

### 7. Passport

Provide a clear scan of the identification page and any other pages requested by the university.

### 8. Course curriculum and course descriptions

Universities may request **ders içerikleri**, a curriculum, or detailed course descriptions. These documents explain:

- the courses in your current program;
- the subjects covered in each course;
- the credit or AKTS value;
- weekly hours; and
- learning outcomes.

The destination university uses these materials to evaluate course equivalency and determine which courses can be recognized.

Course descriptions may be available through your university’s Bologna information system, faculty website, or student affairs office.

### 9. Language-proficiency document

Applicants transferring into an English-medium program may need to prove English proficiency.

Accepted evidence varies by university. Common possibilities include:

- **TOEFL**;
- **PTE Academic**; or
- the destination university’s own English proficiency examination.

Many universities do not accept every internationally known test. IELTS, Duolingo English Test, SAT, or other examinations may be rejected unless they appear explicitly in the university’s equivalency table.

The university may also impose:

- a minimum score;
- a validity period; and
- additional conditions for students transferring from a Turkish-medium program.

For a Turkish-medium program, you may be asked to demonstrate Turkish proficiency. A YTB student who has completed the required TÖMER year may already have suitable documentation, but this should be confirmed with the university.

### 10. High-school equivalence document

The **denklik belgesi** confirms that your secondary-school education is equivalent to the relevant level in Türkiye.

It may not be required for every transfer application, but it is useful to complete the process early because it may be requested later. Information and applications are available through the Ministry of National Education’s [e-Denklik system](https://edenklik.meb.gov.tr/).

---

These are common requirements, not a universal checklist. A university may ask for additional documents, certified translations, photographs, application forms, residence documents, or faculty-specific materials.

## What happens to your scholarship?

tl;dr: A transfer may be possible when you remain in the same program, avoid a semester loss, and obtain the necessary approval and documentation from YTB.

The scholarship authority and the university are separate institutions. Admission to a new university does not automatically transfer or preserve your scholarship, and the university cannot independently guarantee YTB approval.

Before making irreversible changes, inform the relevant YTB or Türkiye Scholarships contact that you intend to apply for yatay geçiş. Explain that:

- you are applying to the same or an equivalent program;
- you intend to continue without losing a semester; and
- you will provide the destination university’s official admission and placement documents.

After admission and course-equivalency evaluation, YTB may require a formal letter from the destination university confirming matters such as:

- your admission as a transfer student;
- your program;
- your year or semester of placement;
- your status as a scholarship student, where applicable; and
- whether the transfer creates a semester loss.

:::important Obtain written approval
Do not withdraw from your current university or complete final registration elsewhere based only on an informal conversation. Ask YTB and the destination university what written approvals are required and in what order the steps must be completed.
:::

## İntibak and course equivalency

tl;dr: Your completed courses must be sufficiently similar to the destination university’s courses. Credit and AKTS values are also considered during the equivalency process.

**İntibak** is the process through which the destination university determines:

- which completed courses are equivalent;
- which grades or credits will be transferred;
- which courses must be repeated; and
- the year or semester in which you will be placed.

The university may compare course titles, but titles alone are rarely enough. It may examine:

- detailed course content;
- learning outcomes;
- weekly teaching hours;
- laboratory components;
- local credit values; and
- AKTS values.

If a course is recognized, you normally do not need to take it again. If it is not recognized, you may need to repeat it at the destination university.

A large number of unrecognized courses can delay graduation. For a scholarship student, that delay may also affect whether YTB approves the transfer.

:::important Confirm your administrative placement
Course recognition does not automatically mean that you have been admitted into the correct year. Ask for written confirmation that you are registered as a yatay geçiş student in the appropriate semester, rather than as a new first-year student.
:::

Before applying, compare the two curricula yourself. A simple table containing your course, the destination course, content overlap, credits, and AKTS can help you estimate the risk of semester loss.

## English proficiency

tl;dr: Check the destination university’s accepted examinations, minimum scores, validity rules, and yatay geçiş examination dates. If an external certificate is not accepted, you may need to take the university’s own examination in person.

This section is relevant to students applying to an English-medium program.

Some universities may not accept transfers from a Turkish-medium program into an English-medium program. Others may accept the transfer but refuse to recognize courses completed in Turkish. Either outcome can make the transfer impractical, so investigate this issue before applying.

If the university allows the transfer, you will still need to meet its language requirement. TOEFL and PTE Academic commonly appear in university equivalency tables, but acceptance is institution-specific.

Many universities impose a validity period on language certificates. The informal guide uses two years as a practical rule of thumb, but the official university regulation or current announcement is the only reliable standard.

If you do not have an accepted certificate, you may be able to take the university’s own proficiency examination through its **Yabancı Diller Yüksekokulu** or equivalent language school.

These examinations may:

- be held only in person;
- take place in a different city;
- be scheduled before, during, or after the transfer application period; and
- conflict with examinations at other universities.

Make sure you locate the examination specifically intended for yatay geçiş applicants. It may be different from the general proficiency examination held for newly admitted first-year students.

:::important Monitor announcements closely
Transfer and language-examination dates may be published only a few weeks before the event. Check the university, faculty, student-affairs, academic-calendar, and language-school pages regularly.
:::

## Collecting reliable information

tl;dr: Prioritize current, first-party sources. University rules change frequently, and unofficial websites may be incomplete or outdated.

Use the following sources for different parts of the process:

- **University yatay geçiş yönergesi:** search the university’s name together with “kurumlar arası yatay geçiş yönergesi.”
- **Current application announcement:** check the official university, registrar, student-affairs, faculty, or senate pages.
- **Application dates:** consult the yatay geçiş announcement and academic calendar.
- **Required documents:** use the current announcement rather than an old checklist.
- **Language-examination dates:** check the university’s Yabancı Diller Yüksekokulu or equivalent language-school page.
- **Program and base-score data:** use [YÖK Atlas](https://yokatlas.yok.gov.tr/).
- **Transfer quotas:** use the relevant [YÖKSİS yatay geçiş quota page](https://yoksis.yok.gov.tr/websitesi/kontenjan/yatayGecis.zul) and the university’s announcement.
- **National regulation:** consult the current official legislation through [Mevzuat Bilgi Sistemi](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/mevzuat?MevzuatNo=13948&MevzuatTur=7&MevzuatTertip=5).

:::tip Verify dates and versions
A document on an official domain can still be outdated. Check its publication date, academic year, amendment history, and whether a newer announcement has replaced it.
:::

When contacting a university, ask precise questions. For example:

- Which document should an international YTB student submit instead of an ÖSYS or YKS result?
- Can a student transfer from a Turkish-medium program into this English-medium program?
- Which language certificates are accepted, and how long are they valid?
- Will I be admitted directly into my current year or semester?
- When is course equivalency assessed?
- Which quota applies to an international student currently enrolled in Türkiye?

Keep copies of replies, especially when they clarify an exception not stated in the published rules.

## The application process

Once you have identified the applicable rules, dates, quota, and required documents, follow the application method specified by the university. Applications may be submitted through:

- an online university portal;
- an electronic form;
- email;
- physical delivery; or
- another system stated in the announcement.

Enter all information carefully and make sure uploaded documents are readable, complete, and current. Barcode-verifiable e-Devlet documents may allow the university to confirm information through connected public systems.

Some applications may allow you to include extracurricular activities. Where relevant, prioritize activities completed during university, but earlier achievements may also be useful. Provide evidence such as:

- certificates;
- project links;
- competition results;
- publications;
- photographs; or
- official letters.

You may also be asked for a statement of purpose, motivation letter, or letter of intent. These terms often refer to a similar document. A strong letter should explain:

- why you want to transfer;
- why the destination university and program fit your goals;
- what you have achieved academically or outside the classroom;
- why you are leaving your current university without attacking it; and
- how you will contribute to the destination institution.

Keep the letter specific, evidence-based, and concise. The [College Essay Guy](https://www.collegeessayguy.com/) website may be useful for general guidance on personal and motivation statements.

:::note English-medium applicants
The university’s language examination may occur before the application, during evaluation, or after conditional admission. Record every relevant date and confirm whether missing the examination automatically invalidates the application.
:::

After submission, save:

- the completed form;
- all uploaded documents;
- the application number;
- confirmation emails; and
- screenshots of the final submission page.

Results are often announced within a relatively short period, but the timeline varies by university.

## After admission

tl;dr: Confirm the result, course equivalency, semester placement, scholarship approval, registration deadline, and withdrawal sequence before leaving your current university.

:::warning This section is still developing
The source guide was written before the author completed the post-admission process. Treat this section as a planning checklist and verify every step with the relevant institutions.
:::

After receiving an offer:

1. Read the result announcement and registration instructions carefully.
2. Confirm whether the offer is final, conditional, or subject to a language examination.
3. Obtain the intibak or course-equivalency decision.
4. Confirm your year and semester placement in writing.
5. Ask the destination university for the letter required by YTB.
6. Submit the documents to YTB and obtain the necessary approval.
7. Confirm the correct sequence for withdrawing from your current university and registering at the new one.
8. Complete registration before the stated deadline.

Do not assume that acceptance alone resolves your scholarship or academic placement.

After registration, you may also need to handle:

- dormitory arrangements;
- address registration;
- migration or residence-permit records;
- student-card issuance;
- health-insurance or administrative records;
- transfer of scholarship records; and
- adaptation to a new university, cohort, and city.

Keep digital and printed copies of every approval, letter, transcript, equivalency decision, and registration document.

---

Best of luck with your applications. Approach the process carefully, verify every important claim at its source, and keep realistic alternatives available.
